Revibing HCI

Agentic Reimplementation as Community Practice

Agentic coding tools can now reimplement the systems described in research papers. This workshop brings the UIST community together to try it, to test ways of verifying the results, and to form early norms for the practice.

    Background

    Revibing Code from Papers: Reimplementing HCI Artifacts

    Software artifacts for most technical HCI projects are unavailable, which makes it hard to extend prior work, use strong baselines, or replicate results. Adar et al. show that agentic tools can reimplement interactive software directly from the paper describing it.

    10UIST systems revibed, none with a public implementation
    27reimplementations, across Cursor, Gemini and Claude
    94.1%mean revibeability, 80% lowest, 100% highest

    Where it fails. Features needing a model or library that is no longer available, drag and drop that would mean rewriting the underlying visualization library, agents building things the paper listed as future work, and agents writing their own prompts even when the paper published them.

    Ten systems, graded test by test

    Each strip is one system's best run, graded against a rubric built from its own paper. Spellburst, Vizability and XCreation came back complete. Per-test detail stands in until the published rubrics are added.

    A paper, and what came back

    Mean revibeability by tool

    Across every run of every system, not only the best one. The same three tools built all twenty seven reimplementations.

    Workshop program

    Interactive group activities that move from building revibes in the morning to evaluating and reflecting on them in the afternoon. Coffee at 10:30 and 15:00.

    9:00 to 9:20

    Welcome and Framing

    9:20 to 9:40

    Lightning Introductions

    9:40 to 11:10

    Live Revibe Demo and Workshop

    A step by step walkthrough of revibing one UIST system paper, chosen from participant nominations. You follow along in groups of two or three, brainstorm remixes while the agents run, then add your own extension. Pre-revibed codebases are on GitHub as a fallback.

      11:10 to 12:15

      Revibes Showcase

      Each group presents a short walkthrough of their revibe, the challenges and opportunities they met, and any remixes they made. Participants vote on the most faithful revibe and the most interesting remix.

      12:15 to 13:45

      Lunch

      13:45 to 14:45

      Evaluation Jam

      Thirty minutes brainstorming validation approaches seeded by organizer-provided contexts and methods, then thirty minutes clustering the post-its at a shared wall into an evaluation catalog.

      14:45 to 16:30

      Breakout Meta Discussions and Cross-Table Exchange

      Two rounds. Each is a forty minute discussion at an organizer-moderated table, then a cross-table exchange where organizers summarize their group. You shuffle tables between rounds.

        16:30 to 17:00

        Closing Synthesis

        Three things the day has to produce

        Each one starts in a session and is finished by the people who were there. All three are mirrored in a public GitHub repository.

        A community documentThe norms this practice needs, drafted in the meta discussions and co-authored by the room.
        An interactive showroomThe revibed systems from the day, collected and running wherever they can run.
        An evaluation catalogA reusable set of ways to check a revibe, clustered from the evaluation jam.

        Apply to participate

        Apply through a short form with a few open-ended questions: which classic HCI system paper you would want revibed, one idea for evaluating a revibed system, and any seed topics for discussion.

        Attendance is through the UIST 2026 registration system. The workshop is in person only and capacity is limited by the venue.
